Your collection

Everything on your shelf, by its label.

CDs you've ripped and ones you haven't, vinyl you may never digitize, and albums you bought as downloads: each one is an item in your collection, with its edition, when you bought it and what you paid, linked to its digital copy when there is one.

A label on every case

CDs and records each have their own series, both from 1. The number sits on a white strip across the case's hinge or down the sleeve's edge, so CD #12 and Vinyl #12 never look alike, and a number already taken is refused.

Find by Label

Type a number and see what it is and what sits next to it on the shelf, so you can go and grab it. On the Mac (⌘L), the iPhone, the Apple Watch (even offline) and with Siri.

Add by barcode

Scan or type the barcode on the case, or a few words, and pick the edition from MusicBrainz. Add & Next goes through a stack in one sitting; albums already in your library can be added in bulk.

Ripping links it

Insert a CD and the app says which one it is; the rip is linked to it. Catalog a CD without ripping it, mark a needle-drop as your record's digital copy, and see what's left in To Digitize.

Browse and search it

Shelves of cases and sleeves, item pages with the track list (a record's with its sides), and search keys like own:vinyl, ripped:no and sticker:cd100-199.

Count it

The Collection pane of Insights: how much is digitized, free label numbers, spending by medium over time, cost per play, best value and what's still in the wrapper.

Import & rip

Import anything. Keep it tidy.

Drop in files and folders. Each track is matched against MusicBrainz, optionally by audio fingerprint via AcoustID. Confident matches are applied automatically; the rest wait in the Import queue with candidates to pick from.

  • Rich metadata and credits: players, producers, engineers, writers
  • Cover art from the Cover Art Archive, or your own image
  • Insert a CD and it's identified by its table of contents, then ripped to FLAC and checked bit for bit with AccurateRip
  • Your original files are never modified

Albums & artists

Pages worth reading.

Album pages show the full release track list, including what you're missing, plus descriptions, community ratings, genres, credits, release details and links to reviews. Artist pages bring photos, bios, stats, links to dozens of sites and upcoming concerts near you.

All of it is fetched once from MusicBrainz, Wikipedia, Wikidata and Last.fm, and kept locally.

Search

Search that understands music.

Plain words search titles, artists, albums, genres, composers, labels and credits. Filters narrow it down, with autocomplete, typo correction, top hits and one-click refinements. Save any search as a smart playlist that keeps itself up to date.

Music DNA

Follow the people behind the music.

See who played, wrote and produced with whom across your library. Click a bubble to put that person at the center, choose how many levels of connections to show, and click your way from one collaborator to the next. Any name in the app opens it.

A real play queue

Play Next and Add to Queue from any song, album, artist or playlist. Drag to reorder; the queue survives a restart. The player expands into a full Now Playing view.

Playlists, smart and not

Arrange regular playlists yourself, or define smart ones with a search. Liked Songs is built in.

History and Last.fm

Plays and time listened per song, album and artist, with optional scrobbling and an offline queue.

Concerts near you

Upcoming shows by the artists in your library, from Ticketmaster and JamBase, including small clubs and independent venues.

Shop for CDs

Pin down the exact edition on MusicBrainz, then compare new and used copies on Discogs, eBay, CDandLP and Alibris, with shipping, in one list. No accounts needed.

Take it with you

Copy albums, artists and playlists to a USB player or drive in the library's own layout, with .m3u playlists. Nothing is ever overwritten.

Fix missing covers

Pick one from the Cover Art Archive, drop in an image or paste one, and optionally embed it in the album's files.

Your public library's CDs

Your next favorite album might be free.

Browse the music CDs of a public library catalog run on Aspen Discovery, like the Minuteman Library Network, with the libraries that own each one and what's on the shelf right now.

  • The whole catalog downloaded and searched on your Mac: genre:jazz decade:60s -is:owned is:available
  • Each CD matched on MusicBrainz by barcode for its cover, track list, rating and description
  • 30-second previews from Apple Music, no account needed
  • Your checkouts, and CDs added to your library lists for the next visit
  • Marked when it's already in your library, even when the catalog spells it differently

iPhone

Your whole library, in your pocket.

Browse everything on your iPhone: your collection with Find by Label and item pages, and the Mac's album and artist pages, Music DNA and Insights. Download what you want over Wi-Fi from the phone itself and play it offline, through a USB DAC at each song's own sample rate, with gapless playback.

Download on the App Store

Two-way Wi-Fi sync

Pair once with a code. Your collection comes along read-only, likes and plays come back to the Mac, and syncs are encrypted with a key only your devices know.

Hi-res on the go

16/44.1, 24/96, 24/192: Now Playing shows the file's format and the rate your DAC is running at.

Same search, same scrobbles

Playlists, Liked Songs and Last.fm scrobbling, including what you play offline.

Widgets & Apple Watch

A glance away.

Home and lock screen widgets show what's playing, with controls that work without opening the app, how much you've listened lately, your streak and top albums, what you added to your collection with your next labels, and a favorite to rediscover. The Apple Watch app is a remote for the iPhone's player that also finds a label on your shelf with the Digital Crown, with complications for listening time, the song playing and your collection.

Privacy

Your library stays yours.

The library, your listening history and your settings stay on your Mac, in one folder you can copy to a new Mac. No sign-up, and no cloud service of its own.

The app only talks to services to look things up: MusicBrainz, the Cover Art Archive and Wikipedia, plus AcoustID, Last.fm, Ticketmaster or JamBase only if you add a key. API keys, secrets and your library PIN live in your keychain.

The iPhone keeps its music out of iCloud backups and syncs with your Mac over your local network, encrypted with a key the two exchange when pairing.

Get it

Install in a minute.

MacmacOS 14 or later

  1. Download MusicLibrary-<version>-macOS.zip from the latest release.
  2. Unzip it and drag MusicLibrary.app to Applications.
  3. Open it. It's signed and notarized, and includes ffmpeg: nothing else to install.
