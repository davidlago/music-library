Physical Collection

Everything on your shelf, by its label.

CDs you've ripped and ones you haven't, and records you may never digitize: each is an item with its own page, its edition, when you bought it and what you paid, linked to its digital copy when there is one.

A label on every case

CDs and records each have their own series, so CD #12 and Vinyl #12 never look alike. The next number is suggested, and a taken one is refused.

Find by Label

Type a number and see what it is and what sits next to it. On the Mac (⌘L), the iPhone, the Apple Watch (even offline) and with Siri.

Add by barcode

Scan or type the barcode, pick the edition from MusicBrainz or Discogs, and go through a stack with Add & Next. Ripping a CD links it on its own, and Sync to Discogs adds it to your collection there.

Digital Collection

Import, rip, digitize. Keep it tidy.

Every song is matched on MusicBrainz, optionally by its sound with AcoustID, tagged with rich credits and cover art, and filed neatly. Your original files are never modified.

  • Insert a CD: it's identified, ripped to FLAC and checked bit for bit with AccurateRip, and a misread track is read again and repaired
  • Digitize your records: record from your turntable at up to 24-bit / 192 kHz, cut the recording into tracks on its waveform, save them as FLAC
  • Credits for every player, producer and engineer, and covers from the Cover Art Archive or your own image

New · Radio DJ

A DJ who knows your records.

Press On Air on any page and a host you name tells you the story behind what you're looking at: who made it, who played on it, how it connects to everything else you own. Ask anything; the DJ checks the web, shows its sources and searches your own collection to answer. Powered by Claude (or any model on OpenRouter), with your own API key.

It knows where you are

An album, an artist, a CD on your shelf, Insights, a playlist: the DJ starts from the page and stays on the air while you browse.

It searches your collection

"What else do I have by them, most played first?" The search runs on your device; only the rows it finds are sent.

Show it a photo

Snap a record or CD in a shop with your iPhone: the DJ tells you what it is, whether you have it, what critics thought and what it's worth.

Everything's a link

What you own opens its page or plays; songs you don't own play a preview; artists you don't know link to a checked Wikipedia article. On iPhone too.

Privacy

Your library stays yours.

Your library, your listening history and your settings stay on your Mac, in one folder you can copy to a new Mac. No account, no ads, no tracking, and no cloud service of its own.

The app talks to MusicBrainz, the Cover Art Archive and Wikipedia only to look things up, and to Last.fm, Ticketmaster, JamBase, Claude or OpenRouter only with your own keys, which live in your keychain.

The iPhone keeps its music out of iCloud backups and syncs with your Mac over your local network, encrypted with a key the two exchange when pairing. The details.

Get it

Install in a minute.

MacmacOS 14 or later

  1. Download MusicLibrary-<version>-macOS.zip from the latest release.
  2. Unzip it and drag MusicLibrary.app to Applications.
  3. Open it. It's signed and notarized, and includes ffmpeg: nothing else to install.
