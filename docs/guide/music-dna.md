# Music DNA

**Music DNA** (Explore) shows the people behind your music and how they connect: who played, wrote and produced with
whom, across both your collections.

![Music DNA centered on Roy Orbison: Jeff Lynne, Tom Petty, T-Bone Burnett, Jim Keltner and dozens of others around him, colored by performance, writing and production](../screenshots/music-dna.png)

## How to open it

- Pick anyone from **Find anyone in your credits**, or browse the grid of people.
- Click a name anywhere in the app: album credits, Now Playing, an artist page's **Music DNA** button, or the Radio DJ.

## Reading the map

The person you chose is in the middle, surrounded by everyone they share songs with in your library; choose how many
**Levels** of connections to show (1 to 3). Colors say how someone's credited: blue for performance, green for writing,
orange for production. Bigger circles mean more shared songs.

Click a bubble to put that person in the middle. The panel beside the map lists their roles, **Top collaborators** (with
how many songs you have together) and their songs in your library, with **Play All** and **Artist Page**.

## Where the connections come from

People come from the credits on your music (players, writers, producers, engineers, from MusicBrainz and the tags) and
the track artists, merged by their MusicBrainz identity so the same person with two spellings is one bubble. Credits
that aren't musical, like photography or design, are left out.

CDs and records with no digital copy add their songs' MusicBrainz credits too, so a session player you only have on
vinyl links to the same people as your music files. Their songs are listed with the copy they're on.

The map is rebuilt whenever your library changes, and is on the iPhone too (Insights ▸ Explore ▸ Music DNA).
